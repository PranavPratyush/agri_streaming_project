## Section 1: District Agricultural Profile & Rainfall Context (Kolar)

Kolar district falls in the **Eastern Dry Agro-Climatic Zone (Zone 5)** of Karnataka:
- **Mean Annual Rainfall:** Approximately 740 mm distributed over 45 rainy days.
- **Rainfall Distribution:** South-West Monsoon contributes ~54% (June–September); North-East Monsoon contributes ~32% (October–December); Pre-monsoon showers contribute ~14% (April–May).
- **Major Constraints:** Highly erratic rainfall onset, prolonged dry spells of 2 to 4 weeks during July–August, high evaporative demand ($> 5\ mm/day$ in pre-monsoon and summer), shallow soil depth over granite gneiss parent rock, low organic carbon content ($< 0.5\%$).

---

## Section 2: Contingency Strategies for Mid-Season Drought in Finger Millet

When the dry spell lasts for 2 to 3 weeks during the vegetative and tillering stage:
- **Inter-cultivation:** Carry out repeated shallow inter-cultivations using country plough or bladed hoes to eliminate weeds and conserve sub-surface moisture.
- **Top Dressing Postponement:** Postpone nitrogenous fertilizer top dressing until soil moisture is replenished by subsequent rain. Applying urea under dry soil conditions causes ammonia volatilization and leaf scorch.
- **Protective Watering:** Provide one protective irrigation (25–30 mm) from farm ponds or borewells through sprinkler/drip systems, focusing strictly on high-value seed production or flowering parcels.

---

## Section 3: Terminal Drought Management in Kolar Ragi

When rainfall ceases early during grain development (late September to October):
- Collect runoff water in farm ponds and apply life-saving irrigation at grain development stage.
- If crop condition indicates complete failure of main earheads due to terminal drought, harvest the green biomass as nutritious fodder for cattle to salvage farm economic value.
- Clear field residues after harvest to prepare the land for early rabi crops or winter horsegram if late showers occur.
