## Section 1: Water Requirements and Irrigation Scheduling

Finger millet is an efficient user of water with total seasonal consumptive water use varying between **350 mm and 500 mm**, depending on seasonal climate, solar radiation, and variety duration:
- Daily water consumption peaks during the transition from vegetative tillering to panicle initiation ($4.0 - 5.5\ mm/day$).
- Finger millet cannot withstand prolonged water stagnation or waterlogging. Free-standing water for more than 24 hours causes root hypoxia, yellowing of foliage, and stunting.
- Good drainage must accompany any supplemental irrigation system, especially in soils with impermeable subsurface murrum layers.

---

## Section 2: Critical Stages for Water Supply

When irrigation water availability is constrained, water must be concentrated strictly at the critical physiological stages:
1. **Life Irrigation (3 to 4 days after sowing / transplanting):** Essential to ensure uniform root strike and seedling emergence.
2. **Vegetative Stage (20 to 25 days):** Stimulates productive primary tillers.
3. **Panicle Initiation & Flowering (50 to 65 days):** The most drought-vulnerable stage in finger millet. Water deprivation at heading causes incomplete panicle exertion and chaffy earheads.
4. **Grain Development Stage (75 to 90 days):** Sustains starch accumulation in seeds.

---

## Section 3: Moisture Conservation & Drainage Rules

- In rainfed black and red sandy loams, avoid over-irrigation. Water applications should not exceed 40 mm per event to prevent deep percolation and nutrient leaching below the active 40 cm root zone.
- Construct earthen bunds around parcel boundaries to retain monsoon showers and minimize overland runoff.
- During harvest stage (last 15 days), withhold irrigation entirely to promote natural ripening, avoid grain mold, and facilitate mechanized or manual reaping.
