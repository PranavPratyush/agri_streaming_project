## Section 1: Agro-Meteorological Drought Dynamics in Rainfed Millets

In the semi-arid Deccan plateau and southern dry tracts (including Kolar and Rayalaseema), rainfall distribution during the South-West and North-East monsoons is characterized by high intra-seasonal variability. Dry spells are categorized as:
- **Early Season Dry Spell (Seedling Stage):** Characterized by prolonged absence of rain after germination. Results in seedling mortality and sub-optimal population density.
- **Mid-Season Dry Spell (Vegetative to Tillering):** 2 to 3 weeks of continuous rain deficit with high ambient temperature and vapour pressure deficit ($VPD > 2.2\ kPa$). Causes leaf rolling, stomatal closure, and reduction in tiller production.
- **Terminal Drought (Flowering to Grain Fill):** Premature cessation of monsoon rains while grain filling is underway. Represents the most damaging stress scenario for finger millet.

## Section 2: Physiological Response & Moisture Stress Indicators

Finger millet exhibits adaptive C4 drought avoidance mechanisms:
- **Stomatal Conductance Regulation:** Rapid reduction in transpiration when available root zone soil moisture drops below 40% of Field Capacity.
- **Osmotic Adjustment:** Accumulation of proline and soluble sugars maintains turgor pressure during mild to moderate stress.
- **Visual Stress Indicators:**
  - Inception of stress: Midday wilting and upward leaf rolling between 11:30 AM and 2:30 PM, with recovery by evening.
  - Moderate stress: Permanent leaf rolling persisting into late afternoon; lower leaf chlorosis and premature leaf tip burning.
  - Severe stress: Extensive drying of lower 3 to 4 leaves; failure of flag leaf to emerge; head blast vulnerability drastically increases due to tissue desiccation.

## Section 3: Non-Chemical Contingency Mitigations for Rainfed Farmers

When environmental telemetry indicates severe moisture stress ($ET_0 > 5.5\ mm/day$, $VPD > 2.5\ kPa$, and zero precipitation for $> 14$ days):
1. **Protective Supplemental Irrigation:**
   - Where harvested water exists in community or individual farm ponds (*Krishi Honda*), prioritize application at **flowering stage**. A single protective irrigation of 20 to 25 mm depth via micro-sprinklers or furrow flood can salvage 60 to 70% of potential yield.
2. **Crop Canopy Management:**
   - Thinning: Remove alternate plant rows or stunted tillers in densely populated stands to reduce total transpirational demand by 30–40%.
3. **Soil Dust Mulch:**
   - Execute shallow inter-tillage using cycle hoes or peg tooth weeders to create a 2–3 cm loose soil layer that retards upward soil moisture evaporation.
4. **Foliar Hydration (Non-Pesticide):**
   - Spraying plain water or 1–2% potassium chloride (KCl) solution during late afternoon hours assists in stomatal regulation and reduces heat shock without hazardous synthetic chemicals.
