## Section 1: Variety Characteristics and Agro-Climatic Suitability

Finger millet (*Eleusine coracana* L. Gaertn), locally termed Ragi, is the predominant food grain in Zone 5 of Karnataka. Variety **GPU-28** is an improved high-yielding, blast-tolerant cultivar recommended for dryland and protective irrigated conditions:
- **Duration:** 115 to 125 days (medium maturity group).
- **Yield Potential:** Grain yield 35 to 45 quintals per hectare under managed conditions; straw yield 60 to 75 quintals per hectare.
- **Canopy & Phenology:** Erect tillering habit, synchronized maturity, semi-compact curved earheads.
- **Grain Quality:** Light brown round grains, rich in calcium (340 mg/100g) and dietary fiber.
- **Stress Tolerance:** Moderate tolerance to intermittent moisture stress; resilient in red sandy loam soils with moderate water-holding capacity.

## Section 2: Soil Profile & Water Management in Red Sandy Loams (Kolar)

The predominant soil series in Kolar district consists of red sandy loam to red loam (Alfisols). These soils are characterized by:
- Texture: Coarse to fine sand (50–65%), silt (15–20%), clay (15–25%).
- Bulk Density: 1.45 to 1.60 g/cm³.
- Soil Depth: Variable from shallow (30–45 cm) on uplands to moderately deep (60–90 cm) in mid-slopes.
- Volumetric Field Capacity ($\theta_{FC}$): 0.18 to 0.22 $m^3/m^3$ (18–22%).
- Permanent Wilting Point ($\theta_{WP}$): 0.08 to 0.10 $m^3/m^3$ (8–10%).
- Available Water Capacity ($AWC = \theta_{FC} - \theta_{WP}$): 0.10 to 0.12 $m^3/m^3$ (100–120 mm per meter depth).

Due to the limited water retention capacity of red sandy loam soils, dry spells exceeding 12 to 14 days during active vegetative and flowering stages deplete root zone available moisture beyond the critical threshold (55% depletion), causing stomatal closure, leaf curling, and delayed earhead emergence.

## Section 3: Critical Stages and Irrigation Guidance

Finger millet exhibits varying vulnerability across its physiological phases:
1. **Establishment & Seedling Phase (Day 0 to 20):** High root development, moderate water requirement ($1.5 - 2.5\ mm/day$). Drought during emergence reduces plant stand.
2. **Vegetative & Active Tillering Phase (Day 20 to 50):** Canopy expands rapidly ($ET_c \approx 3.0 - 4.0\ mm/day$). Moderate moisture deficit slows tillering but plant recovers upon rehydration.
3. **Flowering & Earhead Emergence Phase (Day 50 to 80):** **MOST CRITICAL PERIOD.** $ET_c$ peaks at $4.5 - 5.5\ mm/day$. Moisture stress during anthesis causes pollen sterility, poor seed set, and drastic yield penalty. If protective water is available from farm ponds, provide 25–30 mm supplemental irrigation during dry spells.
4. **Grain Filling & Milking Phase (Day 80 to 105):** Second critical period. Water stress causes grain shriveling and reduced test weight.
5. **Maturity & Senescence (Day 105 to 120):** Crop requires dry ambient conditions for harvest. Irrigation must cease 10 to 12 days prior to harvesting.

## Section 4: Cultural Practices & Soil Moisture Conservation

In rainfed drylands of Kolar without assured irrigation:
- **Inter-cultivation (Edekunte):** Perform 2 to 3 inter-cultivations using bullock-drawn or tractor-drawn hoes at 20, 35, and 50 days after sowing. This creates a soil dust mulch, breaks capillary channels, eliminates weed competition, and reduces evaporative water loss.
- **Conservation Furrows:** Open dead furrows at 3.3 to 4.0 m intervals across the slope to harvest in-situ rainwater and recharge the root zone profile.
- **Residue Mulching:** Spread crop residues or straw mulch at 3 to 5 tonnes/ha between rows to buffer soil temperature fluctuations and extend moisture availability by 4 to 6 days during rainless periods.
- **Extension Notice:** System operators and advisory models must not prescribe specific chemical herbicide or pesticide concentrations. If foliar pests or leaf blast appear, refer the farmer immediately to the local KVK or Assistant Director of Agriculture (ADA) office.
