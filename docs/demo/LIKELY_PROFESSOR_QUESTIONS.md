# AgriKafka-Agent: 22 Likely Professor Questions & Technical Answers

**Target Examination:** Stream Processing and Analytics (SPA) & Agentic Artificial Intelligence (AAI) Final Evaluation  
**Project:** AgriKafka-Agent: Fault-Tolerant, Real-Time Agricultural Risk Monitoring and Decision Support  
**Authors:** Aanish Khan, Daksh Chauhan, Pranav Pratyush  

---

### Q1: Why did you choose Kafka KRaft mode instead of traditional ZooKeeper?
**Answer:** KRaft (Kafka Raft Metadata Mode, Kafka 3.8.0) consolidates metadata management directly into Kafka itself. In our single-broker local development and edge resilience profiles, this eliminates the JVM overhead, port conflicts, and failure modes of managing a separate ZooKeeper cluster. Metadata records are logged to an internal quorum topic (`@kafka:9093`), enabling sub-second controller failover and deterministic state recovery (`docs/decisions/ADR-0002-kafka-broker-and-client.md`).

---

### Q2: How are your Kafka topics partitioned and keyed?
**Answer:** Topics carrying field observations, weather context, candidate alerts, and investigation results are partitioned by `field_id` (`num_partitions: 3`, `replication_factor: 1`). Keying by `field_id` guarantees that all chronological readings and candidate alerts for a single agricultural parcel arrive at the exact same partition in strict event-time order. Sensor health events are keyed by `sensor_id` to maintain linear forensic history per physical probe (`infra/kafka/init_topics.py`).

---

### Q3: How do you achieve end-to-end deduplication and prevent duplicate message processing?
**Answer:** We enforce compound idempotency at every stage:
1. Ingestion generates deterministic keys: `f"{station_id}:{sensor_id}:{event_time}"`.
2. PySpark Structured Streaming deduplicates within a 10-minute watermark using `dropDuplicates(["idempotency_key", "event_time"])`.
3. TimescaleDB uses `ON CONFLICT (idempotency_key) DO UPDATE` / `DO NOTHING`.
4. The Notification Worker uses compound keys `f"{alert_id}:{advisory_version}:{recipient_id}:{channel}"`.

---

### Q4: Explain the difference between event time and processing time in your PySpark pipeline.
**Answer:** `event_time` is the UTC timestamp generated by the physical sensor or weather station at the instant of physical measurement. `processing_time` (or `ingestion_time`) is when our pipeline receives and processes the record. In agricultural streaming, telemetry from remote rural fields is frequently delayed by cellular dropouts. If we computed rolling averages on processing time, delayed bursts of readings would distort daily diurnal cycles. We therefore evaluate all tumbling and sliding windows strictly on `event_time`.

---

### Q5: How do you handle late-arriving data and state explosion in Spark?
**Answer:** In `services/stream-processor/src/agri_stream_processor/pipeline.py` (Line 73) and `features.py` (Line 35), we define an explicit watermark: `.withWatermark("event_time", "10 minutes")`. Spark maintains state only for events arriving within 10 minutes of the latest observed watermark. Any late event trailing by more than 10 minutes is dropped from state aggregation to bound memory usage and prevent out-of-memory errors on workstation nodes.

### Q6: What is "natural flatline differentiation" and why does standard IoT anomaly detection fail in agriculture?
**Answer:** Standard industrial anomaly detection flags zero-variance readings as "sensor stuck" faults. In semi-arid agriculture, this produces catastrophic false alarms:
1. Rain gauges legitimately read exactly 0.0 mm for weeks during dry spells.
2. Soil moisture dries down to a stable residual plateau (hygroscopic water bound to soil colloids) where variance approaches zero.
In `services/risk-engine/src/agri_risk_engine/health/detectors/flatline.py` (Lines 60–95), our `FlatlineDetector` implements physical exemption rules: if vapor pressure deficit (VPD) is high and cumulative rain is zero, the stable dry plateau is recognized as genuine soil physics, yielding 0 False Positives on benchmark fixtures.

---

### Q7: How do you distinguish between physical sensor failure and genuine severe drought?
**Answer:** Through multi-variable cross-correlation and sensor health forensics. A physical capacitive probe failure exhibits an impossible step-change (rate of change exceeding $0.08\,\text{m}^3/\text{m}^3/\text{min}$) or drops below the physical air-dry minimum ($<0.05\,\text{m}^3/\text{m}^3$). Conversely, genuine drought exhibits continuous gradual depletion correlated with high diurnal temperatures, elevated solar radiation, and zero precipitation.

---

### Q8: How is data provenance preserved from ingestion to the farmer screen?
**Answer:** Every event carries a mandatory Pydantic `provenance` sub-model specifying `provenance_class` (`physical_sensor`, `weather_station`, `reanalysis`, or `injected_scenario`), `is_synthetic` (boolean), `dataset_origin`, and `raw_payload_hash`. On the Farmer PWA (`apps/farmer-pwa/`), visual badges explicitly label data as either "Ground Soil Probe" or "Regional Weather Station" so farmers know the source of each reading (`docs/architecture/FARMER_PWA_SPEC.md`).

### Q9: Why is the crop risk calculation deterministic instead of using an LLM?
**Answer:** The dual water balance model defined in FAO-56 is a rigorous physical equation:
$$D_{r,t} = D_{r,t-1} - (P_t - RO_t) - I_t - CR_t + ET_{c,t} + DP_t$$
Calling an LLM to evaluate numerical soil water depletion introduces hallucinations and floating-point errors. By keeping the risk engine 100% deterministic (`services/risk-engine/src/agri_risk_engine/risk/engine.py`), we guarantee mathematical reproducibility and precision. The AI agent layer is reserved for context synthesis, retrieval, and linguistic translation.

---

### Q10: Why implement a multi-agent architecture instead of a single LLM with tools?
**Answer:** Monolithic agents with multiple tools suffer from context drift, tool selection ambiguity, and difficulty auditing intermediate reasoning. By separating responsibilities across 6 specialist agents (`sensor_reliability`, `field_context`, `crop_risk_analyst`, `agronomy_knowledge`, `agricultural_advisory`, `critic_safety`), each agent is constrained to a single prompt, schema, and toolset. Intermediate outputs are inspectable and independently testable (`docs/decisions/ADR-0008-multi-agent-investigation-architecture.md`).

---

### Q11: How does the Supervisor coordinate agents without LangGraph?
**Answer:** Our `MultiAgentSupervisor` (`services/alert-investigator/src/agri_investigator/supervisor.py`) is a pure-Python state machine implementing a directed acyclic graph with a single bounded revision loop. State transitions are strictly validated against `VALID_TRANSITIONS` in `state.py`. This avoids the complex abstractions, hidden callbacks, and non-deterministic looping common in heavy agent frameworks.

---

### Q12: How do agents exchange information? Can agents talk directly to each other?
**Answer:** Direct peer-to-peer communication between agents is strictly forbidden by contract. All information passes through the central `InvestigationState` managed by the Supervisor. Messages are wrapped in canonical `AgentMessage` containers with UUIDs, timestamps, and schema version `1.0.0`. An agent consumes verified data from the state and returns typed Pydantic models.

---

### Q13: What happens when the Critic rejects an advisory proposal?
**Answer:** The `CriticSafetyAgent` (`agents/critic_safety.py`) reviews the proposed advisory. If it finds issues (e.g. ungrounded claims or missing timing), it issues a verdict of `revision_requested` with structured feedback. The Supervisor increments `revision_count` and routes the instructions back to the `AgriculturalAdvisoryAgent`. To prevent runaway loops, the revision cycle is strictly capped ($\le 2$). If exceeded, the run terminates with `ABSTAINED_MAX_REVISIONS_EXCEEDED`.

---

### Q14: How does the system prevent hallucinated citations in agricultural advisories?
**Answer:** We enforce a two-stage citation verification pipeline:
1. The `AgronomyKnowledgeAgent` searches the Chroma vector database and returns an `EvidenceBundle` containing real document citation IDs (e.g., `CIT-FAO56-001`, `UASB-POP-RAGI-2022:SEC03:CH01`).
2. Before an advisory is approved, our non-LLM `DeterministicSafetyGate` (`services/alert-investigator/src/agri_investigator/safety.py`, Lines 56–99) checks every cited string against the set of IDs actually retrieved. If an agent cites a document not present in the bundle, the gate immediately blocks the advisory.

### Q15: What documents are in your agronomy knowledge base and how were they curated?
**Answer:** We audited and indexed 5 authoritative extension documents under `knowledge/corpus/`:

### Q16: What embedding model and vector database do you use?
**Answer:** We use `sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2` in ONNX format producing 384-dimensional dense vectors, paired with a local persistent ChromaDB instance (`knowledge/chroma_db/`) using cosine distance HNSW indexing. In quantitative testing across 10 gold-standard queries, it achieved a Mean Recall@3 of 0.80 and MRR of 0.7333 (`docs/evaluation/RAG_RETRIEVAL_EVALUATION.md`).

---

### Q17: What is the primary LLM provider and what is the fallback strategy?
**Answer:** As approved in `ADR-0006`, the primary model is **Google Gemini 2.0 Flash** via the interactions API. If API credentials are not configured, network calls fail, or rate limits are exhausted, our agents automatically execute deterministic rule-based template fallbacks. The system never blocks or crashes on external LLM outages.

---

### Q18: What is your "Zero Autonomous Physical Actuation" invariant?
**Answer:** Under no circumstances will our platform automatically trigger physical irrigation valves, pumps, or chemical sprayers. In semi-arid smallholder farming, false actuation can deplete scarce borewell aquifers or apply incorrect chemicals. All outputs are strictly decision-support recommendations that require human review and physical confirmation (`docs/rules/agronomic-safety.md`).

---

### Q19: Explain the human review lifecycle and how you handle conflicting actions.
**Answer:** Advisories move through 8 formal states: `PROPOSED`, `AWAITING_REVIEW`, `APPROVED`, `REJECTED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED`, `REQUEST_INSPECTION`, `EXPIRED`, `SUPERSEDED`. 
In `services/notification-worker/src/agri_notifications/lifecycle.py`:
- Duplicate identical actions are returned idempotently.
- Conflicting actions on an already finalized (`APPROVED` or `REJECTED`) advisory are rejected with `HTTP 409 Conflict`.
- Submissions for older advisory versions are rejected to prevent race conditions.

---

### Q20: How does farmer feedback loop back into the system without causing catastrophic forgetting?
**Answer:** Structured farmer feedback (`advisory_relevant`, `evidence_useful`, `sensor_issue_reported`, comments) is logged to the `human_feedback` table with explicit linkages to `investigation_id` and `alert_id`. We enforce an **offline learning invariant**: feedback is stored for offline Stage 20 batch evaluation and manual agronomic review; it **never** mutates LLM prompts or threshold values online.

---

### Q21: What metrics do you track in Prometheus and how do you prevent metric explosion?
**Answer:** We defined 28 standardized Prometheus instruments in `packages/shared-python/src/agri_shared/metrics.py`. To prevent high-cardinality memory explosion, metric labels are strictly restricted to low-cardinality enums (`field_id`, `status`, `variable`, `channel`, `alert_type`). Raw UUIDs, idempotency keys, and observation values are strictly forbidden in metric labels.

---

### Q22: What are the current limitations of the platform?
**Answer:**
1. Physical soil moisture telemetry is currently absent in Karnataka on public open networks (we use external station Sarvamangalam for ambient weather and simulate soil probes via historical replay or synthetic generators).
2. The PySpark structured streaming job requires containerized execution under Docker due to Python 3.13 C-extension incompatibilities on Windows host.
3. Live Telegram external dispatch requires manual user activation and real bot credentials.
